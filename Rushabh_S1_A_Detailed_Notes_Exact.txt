RUSHABH'S STORY: S¹, A, AND MY PERSONAL GROWTH
A detailed record of my experiences, feelings, timeline, and current mindset

---

PART 1 — PERSONAL BACKGROUND

1. Basic information

- Date of birth: 9 June 2009.
- School stage: Currently in 11th standard, preparing for the future through competitive-exam studies.
- Personality as a child: Very introverted, shy, and afraid of going to school alone.
- Current self-perception: I feel that I am now in my prime, having improved academically and worked on my appearance.
- Relationship experience: I have developed strong feelings for two girls, S¹ and A, without having a proper conversation with either of them.

I describe myself as an extreme introvert, jokingly calling it “introvert pro max.”

2. Early school experiences

I started school at around five years old, whereas many children around me started at four.

In Grade 3, I began experiencing feelings for a girl and started understanding what having a crush felt like.

At the end of Grade 4, the COVID-19 pandemic disrupted normal school life. Afterward, I changed schools, made new friends, and entered a new phase of life.

These early experiences form the background to my later crushes.

---

PART 2 — S¹: MY FIRST SERIOUS CRUSH

3. How it began

S¹ became my first serious crush in Grade 7.

She was:
- Extroverted and well-known throughout the school.
- Excellent academically.
- Someone I admired despite considering myself an average student at the time.

I developed strong feelings for her, and those feelings lasted approximately three years.

Despite the length of the crush, I spoke to her only a few times across roughly four years of knowing her.

Important fact: S¹ still does not know that I had feelings for her.

I never directly confessed my feelings to her.

4. The hurtful comments in Grade 7

During the early period of my feelings for S¹, I sent her a simple “Hi” message.

She called me “dark” and “donkey-faced” in connection with that interaction.

These comments affected the way I thought about myself.

I have sometimes said that I think I deserved those comments because of the version of myself I was back then. However, that is my interpretation of my younger self, not an objective fact.

A more balanced interpretation is:

- I was young and inexperienced.
- I was introverted and did not know how to navigate romantic feelings.
- Sending a simple greeting was not inherently wrong.
- S¹ was not obligated to reciprocate my feelings, but I did not deserve insults about my appearance.

I can acknowledge my awkwardness as a younger person without deciding that I deserved to be humiliated.

5. How S¹ influenced my self-improvement

After developing feelings for S¹, I started focusing on two things:

1. Studying harder.
2. Improving my appearance.

My original motivation was specifically to make S¹ find me attractive.

I wanted her to see me differently. Her opinion mattered to me, and I hoped that becoming more attractive and academically capable might change how she perceived me.

This was the beginning of an important period of personal change.

Although S¹ motivated these changes, the effort itself was mine.

6. The Grade 10 heartbreak

S¹ eventually entered a relationship with a boy in Grade 10.

That boy was described by others as my “carbon copy” when he entered the school in Grade 9.

This made the situation particularly painful.

My experience involved three overlapping feelings:

- She chose someone else: I had strong feelings for her for years, but she entered a relationship with another boy.
- Three years of hope came to nothing: I had invested emotionally in a possibility that never developed into a relationship.
- Comparison with the other boy: Being told that he resembled me made the situation more personal and could invite questions about why he was chosen instead.

However, an important distinction is that S¹ never knew about my feelings. She did not knowingly choose him over me in a direct competition between us.

Her relationship was not proof that I was inferior to him.

7. My academic comeback

Despite the emotional difficulty of Grade 10, I scored 96% in my board examinations.

I also scored higher than S¹, which I mentioned jokingly as an academic comeback.

This achievement is important because it shows my academic progress. However, my success does not need to be measured against S¹'s marks to be valuable.

What S¹'s chapter taught me

- I can develop strong feelings without expressing them.
- A crush can last years even when there is very little direct interaction.
- I sometimes invest emotionally in someone I barely know personally.
- Other people's opinions about my appearance can affect my self-image.
- I can work hard and improve, even after emotional disappointment.

S¹ was an important part of my development, but my life does not have to remain connected to her.

PART 3 — ENTERING 11TH STANDARD

8. Academic position and future goals

After scoring 96% in Grade 10, I secured admission to the same college for Grades 11 and 12.

My academic plans include:
- Preparing for MHT-CET.
- Studying some CET material while prioritizing ISI preparation more heavily.
- Continuing to improve my reasoning and academic performance.

My teacher recognized my logical thinking, and I began developing a reputation among classmates as the “smart kid.”

This was a meaningful change from my earlier self-image as an average student.

9. The transition from Grade 10 to Grade 11

The transition did not feel completely unfamiliar because I remained in the same school environment with many of the same friends.

However, there were also many new faces.

One of those new students was A.

At first, I did not particularly notice her. My feelings developed later, without me deliberately looking for another crush.

PART 4 — A ENTERS MY LIFE

10. My first impression of A

Eventually, I noticed a girl wearing glasses.

The qualities that caught my attention were:
- Her glasses.
- The mature impression she gave compared with her peers.
- Her frequent smile.
- Her dimples.

My first impression attracted me strongly.

I began developing feelings for her without consciously deciding to develop another crush.

This is an important distinction: I did not initially set out to find someone new after S¹. My interest in A developed as I noticed her.

However, this first impression tells me what attracted me to her; it does not establish her personality in depth or reveal her feelings toward me.

At this stage, my feelings were based primarily on observation rather than direct conversation.

PART 5 — FRIENDS DISCOVER MY CRUSH

11. The boys, the new students, and truth-or-truth

As new girls joined the class, some of the boys began commenting on which girls they found attractive.

The group involved in my story included:
- Rushabh (me).
- Krushna.
- Shreevas.
- Sumit.

We started playing truth-or-dare, but because we were in the middle of a lecture, it became truth-or-truth.

Through these conversations, my friends discovered that I had feelings for A.

This made my crush known to some of the boys, even though I had never spoken directly to A about it.

12. Batch separation and the complaint

Management subsequently placed A and me in different batches.

During breaks, I sometimes went to her batch and spent the break watching her from a distance.

Eventually, someone complained, and we were restricted from going into that batch.

I do not know who made the complaint or whether A herself was involved.

It would therefore be incorrect to claim that A personally complained about me.

Nevertheless, the restriction was a boundary that needed to be respected.

Looking back, spending entire breaks staring at someone could make that person uncomfortable, even if my intentions were not harmful.

The important lesson is that having a crush is not wrong, but my actions must respect the other person's space.

The separation may also have made me think about A more because I could no longer see her as freely during breaks.

PART 6 — G AND H BECOME INVOLVED

13. My attempt to learn about A

At one point, I asked an ex of one of my friends about A.

The response I received was along the lines of:

“She doesn't have internet in all these.”

I did not have enough information to know exactly what that meant or what A's own position was.

A few days later, H, a friend who had not previously known about my crush, approached me dramatically and said something like:

“Hey, you like A, right? I got to know about you.”

I was confused about how H had learned about it.

14. Discovering the connection between H and G

After some investigating, we discovered that H and G were in a relationship.

G was A's best friend.

I then found part of a conversation between G and H.

The exchange was approximately:

G: “Krushna likes S², right?”

H: “How do you know?”

G: “I can read boys' faces.”

G: “Rushabh likes A.”

H: “How? What?”

This suggested that G had identified my crush, possibly from observing my behaviour.

It did not establish whether A had already discussed me with G.

What this conversation establishes

- G knew or strongly suspected that I liked A.
- H learned about my crush through the connection with G.
- G claimed she could read boys' faces.
- My crush was becoming known beyond the original group of boys.

What it does not establish

- That A liked me.
- That A had discussed me romantically with G.
- That G's interpretation of my behaviour was evidence of A's feelings.

G's knowledge of my feelings and A's feelings are two separate things.

---

PART 7 — THE REPORTED ANSWER ABOUT A'S FEELINGS

15. Asking H to find out

After discovering that G knew about my crush, I asked H to ask about A's feelings toward me.

The information that came back was confusing.

According to the report I received through this social chain, A had initially said yes, then no, and had also reportedly described me as a smart boy who should not be doing these things.

The exact meaning and context were unclear.

I did not hear this directly from A.

Therefore, I must distinguish:
- What A may have said.
- What G may have told H.
- What H communicated to me.
- What I understood from that report.

The report cannot be treated as a clear confession of romantic interest or a definitive explanation of rejection.

The “smart boy” comment may have been intended to discourage me from pursuing a relationship, but its precise meaning is unknown.

16. G's statement about the next two years

In a separate conversation, I asked G whether I should continue having hope.

G said:

“You can keep hopes but she don't wanna be in this for next 2 yrs.”

I also asked whether A would go with someone else because I thought I might wait for her.

G replied:

“She won't go.”

The source of that second statement is unclear. It may have reflected A's own words, G's opinion, or G's interpretation.

It must not be treated as a confirmed promise from A that she will remain single.

What I know about this information

- G suggested that A did not want to be in a relationship for the next two years.
- G did not tell me to abandon all hope.
- G also said A would not go with someone else.
- I do not know whether A personally made either statement in those exact terms.

What remains unknown

- Whether A actually wants to avoid relationships for two years.
- Whether she has romantic feelings for me.
- Whether she would want a relationship after Grade 12.
- Whether she would ever choose to pursue anything with me.

I should not build a two-year commitment around uncertain second-hand information.

PART 8 — EYE CONTACT AND SMILES

17. A's behaviour after she may have learned about my crush

After the information about my feelings circulated, I began noticing eye contact and smiles from A.

Over a period of approximately 20–25 days, I noticed moments such as:
- A looking in my direction.
- Me noticing her already looking at me.
- Eye contact followed by a smile.
- Her catching me looking at her.
- Expressions that seemed to me to be knowing or playful.
- Smiles that appeared happy to me.

At times, I felt as if her expression communicated:

“I know everything, buddy.”

That is my interpretation of her expression, not a statement she actually made.

I have described some of her smiles as looking genuinely happy.

However, I cannot know her emotional state with certainty from her facial expression alone.

18. My interpretation of her looking at me

My current theory is that A knows I like her and sometimes looks at me because she expects me to look at her.

I feel she might be checking whether I am looking again rather than necessarily looking because she likes me.

This is a plausible interpretation, but it is not confirmed.

Other possibilities include:
- Curiosity about someone who likes her.
- Awareness of my attention.
- Amusement about the situation.
- Ordinary eye contact.
- Some degree of personal interest.

The observations do not distinguish reliably between these explanations.

My honest position is that I do not know whether A likes me romantically.

PART 9 — THE BIKE INCIDENT

19. Avoiding A for two days

At one point, I deliberately avoided eye contact with A for two days.

My reason was that I wanted to see whether she would notice the change and look at me first.

This was part of an experiment to understand whether she noticed my attention.

20. The moment on my bike

After those two days, I was riding my bike and looked toward A.

She was already looking at me.

I noticed that she smiled while maintaining eye contact.

G was standing beside her, and A was saying something to G while continuing to look at me.

I thought the words might have sounded like:

“He's looking, he's looking.”

However, I was not certain that I had heard those words correctly.

I did not look at G to verify the conversation because my attention was on A.

My reaction

I felt confused.

The moment made me wonder whether A had noticed that I had stopped looking at her and was reacting when I looked again.

I wanted to experience that feeling again, partly because the uncertainty made me curious and excited.

What can reasonably be concluded

- A was looking at me at that moment.
- I noticed a smile.
- She was talking to G.
- I thought I heard a comment about me looking, but I was uncertain.

What cannot reasonably be concluded

- That A definitely noticed my two-day absence.
- That she was definitely talking about me.
- That she missed my attention.
- That she was jealous or romantically interested.

Even if she had said “he's looking,” that would establish awareness of my attention, not necessarily romantic attraction.

I must also keep my attention on the road while riding. Trying to interpret a crush's expressions while on a bike is not worth risking my safety.

PART 10 — THE EYE-CONTACT LOOP

21. Wanting more of the same feeling

After the bike incident, I wanted more of that feeling.

I started paying attention to whether A was looking, catching me looking, or ignoring me.

At the same time, I continued deliberately avoiding eye contact because I wanted to see whether she would notice.

This created a conflict:
- I wanted to experience eye contact.
- I wanted to detach.
- I wanted A to notice my absence.
- I did not want to be the one always looking first.

I began interpreting her behaviour as a sequence of looking, catching me looking, and then ignoring me.

At times, her apparent ignoring felt to me like she was communicating that she was no longer interested.

However, I cannot know whether she was intentionally ignoring me or whether her behaviour had any particular meaning.

22. Why the uncertainty feels compelling

The eye-contact moments are infrequent and unpredictable.

I do not know when A will look at me, whether she will smile, or what her expression means.

That uncertainty can make each moment feel unusually rewarding and encourage me to look for another one.

I miss the eye contact because I had become accustomed to those small interactions, even though we have never spoken directly.

Missing the eye contact is not proof that A likes me, and it is not a reason to keep checking for her attention.

It means that these moments have become emotionally significant to me.

23. Why I am deliberately avoiding eye contact

My original reason was: I wanted to see whether A would notice and look at me first.

I hoped that she might react to my absence or wonder why I was no longer looking.

This is different from avoiding her because I genuinely want to move on.

My avoidance has partly been a test of her reaction.

That test cannot give me a reliable answer about her feelings because:
- She may notice and still not have romantic interest.
- She may not react even if she has some interest.
- She may simply respect the distance I appear to be creating.
- She may not interpret my behaviour as a meaningful change.

A healthier approach is to stop deliberately staring and stop deliberately ignoring her. If our eyes meet naturally, I can behave normally and carry on with my day.

PART 11 — MY FEELINGS ABOUT A

24. Why A matters to me

A attracted me through her appearance, glasses, mature impression, smile, and dimples.

I developed feelings without planning to.

As the eye contact and smiles continued, those moments became part of my emotional experience of liking her.

I have never had a direct conversation with A.

This means that:
- My feelings are real.
- My knowledge of her as a person is limited.
- My interpretation of her feelings is uncertain.
- I have not yet had the experience of getting to know her through an ordinary conversation.

The intensity of my feelings does not automatically tell me how compatible we would be or whether she feels the same way.

25. My comparison between S¹ and A

S¹ was my first serious crush and lasted around three years.

I never told S¹ how I felt.

A is now the second major crush in my story, and I have also never spoken to her directly.

The situations are not identical, but there is a possible pattern: I can develop strong feelings from a distance while having little direct interaction with the person.

I want this chapter to be different in a healthy way.

That does not mean I must confess to A or force a relationship. It means I can learn to communicate naturally, get to know people, and accept that I cannot control another person's feelings.

PART 12 — MY CURRENT DECISION: WAIT, DETACH, AND GROW

26. What I told G

I told G that I would continue having feelings for A until the end of Grade 12.

I find it difficult to stop liking someone who is in front of me every day.

I know I cannot simply switch my feelings off by making a decision.

My current plan is to wait, detach, and grow. I have also acknowledged that I am young and experiencing strong hormonal and emotional changes.

I recognise that my feelings may change as I mature.

27. What “wait” means to me

I am not necessarily trying to stop liking A immediately.

I want to leave the possibility open that something may happen in the future.

However, I need to distinguish between allowing feelings to remain and promising to wait for someone.

A healthy version of waiting means:
- I do not force myself to stop liking her.
- I do not demand that she reciprocate my feelings.
- I do not assume she will become interested in the future.
- I remain open to whatever life brings.
- I do not make promises about my future feelings.

I can remain open to A if she clearly expresses interest someday without putting my life on hold until then.

28. What “detach” means to me

Detachment does not mean pretending that I do not care about A.

It means:
- Not monitoring her every glance.
- Not making every smile into a clue.
- Not testing whether she notices my absence.
- Not treating her attention as a measure of my worth.
- Not letting uncertainty control my mood or studies.
- Not forcing myself to ignore her when our paths naturally cross.

The goal is to let my feelings exist without making them the centre of my daily life.

29. What “grow” means to me

My academic and personal growth began partly because of my feelings for S¹.

I studied harder and worked on my appearance because I wanted her to find me attractive.

Now I want to continue developing myself for reasons that extend beyond whether a particular girl likes me.

My priorities include:
- Maintaining strong academic performance.
- Preparing for MHT-CET.
- Giving greater priority to ISI preparation, as planned.
- Improving my confidence and communication.
- Building friendships and gaining real-world social experience.
- Taking care of my appearance without believing I must change myself to deserve affection.

I scored 96% in Grade 10 and have developed a reputation as someone with good logical thinking.

Those are achievements I can build on independently of my romantic life.

PART 14 — MY GUIDING PRINCIPLES GOING FORWARD

32. How I want to handle A

1. No fake hope. I want honest interpretations, not reassurance based on ambiguous clues.
2. Respect A's choices. Her feelings and decisions belong to her.
3. No more eye-contact experiments. I do not need to ignore her deliberately to test her reaction.
4. No unnecessary staring. I can respect her space while allowing natural interactions.
5. No assumptions from G's opinions. I must distinguish A's actual words from what G or H believes.
6. No promise to wait two years. I can remain open to A without committing my future to an uncertain possibility.
7. Focus on my education. CET and ISI preparation are important parts of my future.
8. Build real communication skills. If a natural opportunity arises, I can speak normally rather than relying entirely on eye contact.
9. Keep growing for myself. My self-worth is not determined by whether A or S¹ finds me attractive.
10. Stay safe while riding. I must keep my attention on the road rather than on whether A is looking at me.

33. My current emotional position

I still like A.

I miss the eye contact and smiles.

I sometimes hope she notices when I stop looking at her.

At the same time, I suspect that she may simply be aware of my crush rather than romantically interested in me.

I do not know which interpretation is correct.

I want to let myself feel what I feel while continuing to detach from the uncertainty and develop as a person.

I am not required to stop liking A immediately, and I am not required to wait for her until Grade 12 ends.

I can allow my feelings to evolve naturally.

FINAL SUMMARY

My story began with an introverted childhood and my first serious crush, S¹. I spent years having feelings for her without telling her, partly motivated by a desire to become more attractive and academically successful. Her relationship with another boy hurt me, but I still achieved 96% in my board examinations and continued developing myself.

In Grade 11, I unexpectedly developed feelings for A. My attraction began with her appearance and smile, and the situation became more emotionally complicated when friends learned about my crush, G became involved, and I received an unclear second-hand report about A's feelings.

Subsequent eye contact and smiles made me hopeful and confused. I started avoiding eye contact to see whether A would notice, but the uncertainty made me want those moments even more.

My current plan is to wait in the sense of not forcing my feelings to disappear, detach from constant interpretation, and grow academically and personally.

My most important conclusion is this: my feelings for A are real, but her feelings remain unknown. I can care about her without assuming she likes me, respect her boundaries without suppressing my emotions, and continue building my future without promising to wait for her.

I want this chapter to help me grow into someone who can experience real connections, communicate more confidently, and value himself independently of whether a crush becomes a relationship.
